# Practical Recommendations for Trekking in Bolivia

## Altitude Acclimatization

La Paz sits at around 3,600-4,000 masl depending on the area, and many trekking routes exceed 4,500 masl. It's recommended to:

- Spend at least 2 days in La Paz before starting any high-altitude trek.
- Stay well hydrated and avoid alcohol during the first few days.
- Know the symptoms of altitude sickness (soroche): headache, nausea, excessive fatigue. If severe, you should descend.
- Coca tea (mate de coca) is a traditional local remedy for relieving mild altitude symptoms.

## Recommended Gear

- Layered clothing: thermal base layer, insulating layer, and windproof/waterproof shell.
- Trekking boots with good grip, ideally already broken in.
- High-factor sunscreen and UV-protective sunglasses.
- Water bottle or hydration system, plus water purification tablets or a filter.
- Headlamp, since some routes involve walking during low-light hours.

## Seasons

- **Dry season (May to September)**: the best time for trekking, with clear skies and dry trails.
- **Rainy season (November to March)**: muddy trails and higher risk of landslides; not recommended for beginners.

## Guides and Agencies

For technical routes (such as Huayna Potosí) or multi-day circuits in remote areas, hiring a certified guide or local agency is strongly recommended.
